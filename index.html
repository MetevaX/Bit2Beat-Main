About Bit2Beat

Bit2Beat is our entry to the NASA Space Apps Challenge “Earth Information Jukebox”. It turns Earth data into sound, so anyone, including people who are blind or have low vision, can follow how our planet changes by listening.

How it works

Every number becomes a note: higher values play higher. Each series is scaled between its own lowest and highest value (or a range shared with series in the same unit) and placed on a musical scale, so equal steps in the data sound like equal steps in the melody. Position in time can also become position in space, from simple stereo to 7.1.4 surround.

The data comes from NASA GISTEMP, NASA POWER and NASA EONET, the USGS earthquake feed and Open-Meteo. When there is no internet, Bit2Beat uses real copies of those feeds saved inside the app. Everything runs in your browser; your files never leave your computer.

Our vision

We as a team enjoyed making this from scratch. We experienced and learned a lot during the time, and we had to know each other well. Every one of us contributed and gave all he had.

Our vision is to help first: free technology like this can open data to people who cannot see a chart, and anyone who tries it for fun learns something about Earth along the way.

How to use Bit2Beat

  1. Pick something to hear. Data has NASA climate series and your own CSV files. Earth events has live earthquakes and natural events, played across the map from west to east. Image turns a picture or a satellite view into sound.
  2. Press Play. Higher values play higher. The Melody panel and the score show the same notes.
  3. Shape the sound. Change instrument, scale and tempo, mute or solo channels, or press Suggest.
  4. Export. Download audio, the notes as CSV or the score.
