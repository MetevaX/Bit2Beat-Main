        How to use Bit2Beat

        Bit2Beat turns Earth data into music: every number becomes a note, and a higher value plays a higher note. Here is every part, in a few words. Open the studio →

        First steps

        1. Pick a tab (Image, Video, Earth events or Data) and a source from the menu next to it.
        2. Press Play (or the Space key). The line on the picture or chart shows where you are.
        3. Change the sound in the Sound card: a mood, an instrument, a scale, the tempo.
        4. Need help? Press Guide (or G): Bitty points at the real buttons. The Story page plays eight chapters of real data.

        The four tabs

        Image
        A picture or map is cut into bands; each band is a note. Choose what to hear (Light, Colour, or Colour key for real values) and how to scan (left to right, top to bottom, radar, or Follow pointer).
        Video
        Two notes a second: the brightness, colour and motion of the picture. The video follows the music.
        Earth events
        Wildfires, storms, floods, ice and earthquakes, played from west to east. Further north is a higher note and a bigger circle; bigger events are louder.
        Data
        Climate series, NASA POWER for any place, the challenge's data sets as numbers, or your own spreadsheet (Upload).

        The Space Apps challenge data (seven NASA data sets) is in every menu and works without the internet.

        Search

        • Type a subject (rain, sea ice, fires): results appear as you type, even with a typo.
        • Each tab finds its own kind of thing: pictures in Image, videos in Video, data sets in Data.
        • File type (Image tab) finds scientific files: GeoTIFF, NetCDF and HDF5.
        • Choose a result to play it. Reload gets the newest data again.

        Listening and reading

        • Now playing (navy card): the step, its note and the real value behind it.
        • What the data says: the trend, highs and lows and sudden jumps in plain words; Read aloud (or D) speaks them.
        • Melody shows every note; Score is the sheet music; Readings (or T) lists every value.
        • Channels: one row per series; M mutes, S plays it alone, ⇅ flips its pitch.
        • Follow pointer (Image tab): move over the picture, or Tab to it and use the arrow keys. You hear the data under the pointer, and Bit2Beat says which data it is, its value and where you are.

        Sound

        • Mood: Default, Sad, Happy or Excited sets the whole sound in one click. Default brings back the starting sound.
        • Smart Tuning picks the scale, tempo and instrument from the shape of the data. Every change can be undone from the message that appears.
        • Space and tone: stereo, headphone 3D, and 5.1, 7.1 or 7.1.4 surround for scientific maps; room, tone and movement.
        • Listening aids: a reference tone, a tick on sudden jumps, and an optional drum beat.

        Scientific files and your own files

        • Upload (top bar) opens any file and picks the tab: spreadsheets (CSV, Excel), pictures, videos, and GeoTIFF, NetCDF or HDF5 maps up to 5 GB.
        • What's inside lists the layers. Listen to the whole map, one spot over time, or a time-lapse; zoom with + and −.
        • Your files stay on your device.

        Without a screen

        • Voice (top right, or V): say play, pause, explain, search for sea ice, result 2, violin, default mood or help, in any language.
        • Accessibility: bigger text, high contrast, dark theme, calmer motion, larger buttons, mono sound.
        • Every control works with the keyboard and a screen reader; captions describe each note.

        Export and share

        • Export: audio (stereo, headphone 3D, surround, Dolby Atmos ADM), the notes as CSV, the score as ABC or PNG, and the score in braille (BRF) for embossers and braille displays.
        • Record captures what you play. Sessions saves your setup.
        • The address bar remembers everything: a shared link opens the same view.

        About Bit2Beat

        Bit2Beat is our entry to the NASA Space Apps Challenge “Earth Information Jukebox”. It turns Earth data into sound, so anyone, including people who are blind or have low vision, can follow how our planet changes by listening.

        How it works

        Every number becomes a note: higher values play higher. Each series is scaled between its own lowest and highest value (or a range shared with series in the same unit) and placed on a musical scale, so equal steps in the data sound like equal steps in the melody. Position in time can also become position in space, from simple stereo to 7.1.4 surround.

        The data comes from the NASA Earth Information Center (its content database of pictures and videos, and its data API of real maps), NASA satellite images (GIBS), NASA GISTEMP, POWER and EONET, and the USGS earthquake feed. The challenge’s own data sets are bundled with the app, and real copies of the feeds are saved inside it, so it works without the internet. Everything runs in your browser; your files never leave your computer.

        Our vision

        We as a team enjoyed making this from scratch. We experienced and learned a lot during the time, and we had to know each other well. Every one of us contributed and gave all he had.

        Our vision is to help first: free technology like this can open data to people who cannot see a chart, and anyone who tries it for fun learns something about Earth along the way.

        How to use Bit2Beat

        1. Find something to hear. Search NASA’s Earth Information Center above the tabs, or pick from the menu (the Space Apps challenge data works offline). Image turns a map or picture into sound, Video follows a NASA animation or time-lapse, Earth events plays natural events and earthquakes across the map, Data plays climate series and your own CSV.
        2. Press Play. Higher values play higher. The Melody panel and the score show the same notes.
        3. Shape the sound. Pick a mood (default, sad, happy or excited), change instrument, scale and tempo, mute or solo channels, or press Smart Tuning.
        4. Export. Download audio, the notes as CSV, the score, or the score in braille.
