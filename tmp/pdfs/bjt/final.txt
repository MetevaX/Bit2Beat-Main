Input resistance, output resistance, and voltage gain

The five-step routine

1. Find the DC bias first, if it is not given. Capacitors are open for DC. Compute IC and verify
   the region of operation. Then use gm = IC /VT and rπ = β/gm .

2. Redraw for midband AC. Ground ideal DC voltage supplies; open ideal DC current sources. Short
   sufficiently large coupling/bypass capacitors. Keep every resistor that still connects two different AC
   nodes.

3. Name the ports. Here vi means the actual voltage at the amplifier input terminal. The source
   voltage vs is usually different because Rs and Rin form a divider.

4. Find loaded voltage gain. Include RL , or the next stage’s input resistance, in the output load.
   Find Av = vo /vi first, then As = vo /vs = Av Rin /(Rs + Rin ) when the input divider applies.

5. Find output resistance independently. Remove the external load, set independent signal sources
   to zero, retain their internal resistances, and apply a test voltage vx . Keep the controlled source:
   Rout = vx /ix .

What setting a source to zero means: an ideal voltage source becomes a short; an ideal current
source becomes an open. A voltage source with series resistance leaves that resistance in the circuit.
A port checklist


      Quantity              What you must include
      Rin                   Everything connected inside the input port, including bias resistors.
                            Exclude the source’s own series resistance.
      Av = vo /vi           The actual output load. Specify whether vi is at base or emitter.
      As = vo /vs           Source attenuation as well as loaded stage gain.
      Rout                  Source resistance after zeroing its voltage. Remove only the external
                            load, not an internal emitter or collector resistor.


Conventions throughout. VT = 25 mV; transistors are forward-active; ro = ∞ unless stated. RB
means the equivalent AC bias resistance, usually R1 ∥ R2 . Figures are midband AC circuits with bias
sources omitted, except the explicitly labeled DC exercise; bypass capacitors are shown where their
connections are being tested. All numerical gains are V/V. Use finite β unless an approximation is
requested.

Interpretation: the collector load halves the stage gain, and the source divider reduces it again. Output
resistance is not RC ∥ RL because RL is removed for that measurement.
Two useful physical checks
Looking into the base: emitter resistance is multiplied by β + 1 because emitter current is β + 1 times
base current.
Looking into the emitter: base-side resistance is divided by β + 1. This is why a follower has low
output resistance and a CB stage has low input resistance.

A follower output-resistance measurement
Remove the external load and set vs = 0. The base is connected to ground through RG = Rs ∥ RB , not
necessarily through a short. A test current entering the emitter is shared between the internal RE and
the transistor path. With ro = ∞, that transistor path has resistance

Ask before every calculation: Which terminal is the input? Which terminal is the output? Which
terminal is at AC ground? Which resistor is the external load?
